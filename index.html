What makes this an agentic workflow?

Observe state → evaluate legal actions → choose an action → update the world → observe again. This director is a transparent rule-based agent. It is not an LLM pretending to be a real NBA executive.

An LLM could explain the recorded decision or propose hypotheses. The arithmetic and state transitions should remain reproducible. Agents act on expected outcomes; they do not see future game results.

Open the model: assumptions, limits, and data needed

This is an educational simulation, not an NBA forecast or a human-subject experiment. All strengths, revenues, costs, preferences, lottery weights and safeguards are invented. The closed league uses a deliberately simple loss-weighted draft lottery, not the NBA’s current rules.

Every team chooses between competing and rebuilding. Rebuilding reduces current strength by 9 and adds 5 next-season development points. A director values a future benefit of 12 plus 0.3 × (100 − strength), versus a present cost of 18. In the top tier, an estimated incremental relegation probability adds an expected financial cost. Lower-tier directors also consider a foregone promotion opportunity. These behavior rules are assumptions to vary, not findings about executives.

Teams play two games against each same-tier opponent. Each game uses a logistic strength-based win probability and a preassigned random draw. In the closed world all teams share a tier. Revenues reflect tier and win rate; costs reflect strength. After results, a one-season transition payment covers 60% of the assumed 50-unit base revenue gap in the protected world. It is funded equally by the five surviving top-tier teams. The same protection rule feeds the next decision.

Draft selection is one weighted random draw among all teams: weight = 1 + 3 × loss rate. A lottery winner gains 10 strength points. All teams also experience development, aging, and the same team-season random shock. Tier movement occurs after the season. Cash does not affect strength in this simple model, so it cannot demonstrate a financial death spiral.

Worlds share the same team/game/season random draws where comparable; schedules and opponents differ by format. Thus differences represent a package of modeling choices. One seeded run is not evidence of a policy effect. A research version needs many seeds, sensitivity analysis, and validation against held-out seasons.

  • Sporting data: historical results, rosters, minutes, injuries, team strength and development trajectories.
  • Financial data: attendance, ticket prices, media and sponsorship income, payroll and operating costs. Public valuation estimates are not annual profit.
  • Behavioral data: fan experiments and stakeholder interviews. Do not infer intent to tank from losses.
  • Rules: dated lottery rules, NBA governance and CBA provisions; EPL/EFL promotion and financial arrangements.

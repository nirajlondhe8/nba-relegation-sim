How an agent becomes useful

Each agent receives defined inputs, applies explicit checks, and returns a finding with provenance and limitations. The challenger rejects unsupported causal claims. The decision agent only recommends the next research stage. A later LLM implementation should use the same schema and require human verification of every source and approval.

Make safeguards testable

Specified safeguard package: two-year transition payments, protected sharing, showcase → protected pilot → full trial.

Add: name the funding source; define floor and payment taper; model recurring cohorts; specify two-tier draft and contract rules; establish rights and labor gates before a trial.

Experimental gap: T2 bundles payments and revenue sharing. T2 versus T1 estimates the bundle’s effect. Separate arms or a factorial design are needed to identify each component’s effect.

A phased recommendation

1. Evidence: documents, stakeholder interviews and downside financial model.

2. Showcase: validate paid demand and operating costs. No inference about behavior under real relegation.

3. Protected pilot: test logistics and revenue-sharing administration after permissions. Protection changes incentives, so it cannot fully test relegation risk.

4. Conditional trial: only after funding, labor, owner and rights requirements are resolved; set stop rules and evaluate behavior over seasons.

Evidence conclusion

The NBA’s June 2024 Constitution provides direct evidence that adoption is a governance problem as well as a competitive-design problem. Article 17(a) generally requires a three-fourths vote of all Governors for amendments, subject to its exceptions and notice requirements; Article 4(d) separately addresses prospective-owner approval. These provisions establish that the research must identify the owner coalition and ownership changes required for a two-tier structure. They do not reveal owners’ preferences or establish the complete legal route for relegation, and later amendments must be checked. Conclusion: adoption feasibility requires document analysis, stakeholder interviews, and a funded downside scenario; positive fan responses alone do not establish adoption readiness. NBA Constitution, printed pp.9 and 32.

Research contribution

Niraj Londhe analyzed NBA-specific adoption barriers using the NBA Constitution (June 2024), the NBPA’s 2023 CBA materials, the NBA’s 2024 media-rights announcement, and Leeds United’s FY2024 accounts. The resulting contribution defines the adoption-focused subquestion, specifies the document-analysis and stakeholder-interview plan, and provides an illustrative safeguard stress test that separates verified evidence from assumptions.
